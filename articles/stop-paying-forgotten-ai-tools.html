Stop Paying for Forgotten AI Tools: Find and Review Unused Subscriptions

A quick inventory can reveal plans that slipped out of your routine. Separate billing dates from usage, then make a deliberate keep-or-cancel decision.

By Stackcutify Team•5 min read•Updated Oct 6, 2026Your list, your decision
On this page

  2. Review usage separately
  3. Prioritize the audit
  4. Cancel with the provider

Find every recurring AI plan

Start with recent payment statements, email receipts, and app-store subscription lists. Search for AI product names as well as payment intermediaries, since the merchant on a statement may not match the product name you remember.

Next, check the plan or billing area of each vendor account. This helps confirm which account owns the subscription, the amount, billing cycle, renewal date, and cancellation route.

A payment record can point you to a subscription, but only the provider account or billing platform can confirm the current plan and renewal status.

Review usage separately from billing

Write down how often you actually use each tool: daily, weekly, monthly, occasionally, or not at all. Do not let a monthly or yearly billing label stand in for usage frequency.

Ask a practical question

When did I last use this subscription for a task I care about? Does another tool already cover the same job? Would I choose to subscribe again today at this price?

Where available, check your own product history. A receipt confirms payment, not usage. Avoid treating a lack of remembered activity as proof until you have checked the account and the way you access the service.

Prioritize a short audit

  1. List each tool, its current charge, billing cycle, and next renewal.
  2. Normalize weekly prices with ×52 ÷12 and yearly prices with ÷12.
  3. Mark your actual usage frequency separately.
  4. Review forgotten tools, overlapping services, and upcoming renewals first.

Keep renewal timing visible

A yearly plan can have a low monthly equivalent but a large renewal charge on one date. Use both values: monthly equivalent for comparing costs and renewal date for managing cash flow.

Cancel through the billing provider

If you decide to stop a plan, use the subscription settings for the account that bills you. If an app store or another billing platform processed the purchase, use that platform’s subscription controls.

Follow the cancellation prompts, save the confirmation, and verify the renewal status or end date. Removing an app or deleting a shortcut does not necessarily stop future charges. Check the provider’s current terms if you are unsure about access or refunds.

Make the decision reversible where possible

Before canceling, export or save anything you need and note when access ends. If you later need the tool again, you can compare the then-current price and terms instead of paying for a plan you are not using now.

FAQ

How do I find AI subscriptions I forgot about?

Review payment statements, email receipts, app-store subscriptions, and the plan pages in your vendor accounts.

How can I tell whether an AI tool is unused?

Check your own activity history where available and think about whether it supported a real task recently. Billing alone does not show usage.

Should I cancel a yearly plan immediately?

Check its renewal date, terms, and remaining access first. If you cancel, save the confirmation and verify renewal has stopped.

What is the quickest way to prioritize subscriptions?

Compare monthly equivalent, usage frequency, renewal date, and overlap. Review forgotten and duplicate plans first.

Does Stackcutify automatically cancel subscriptions?

No. It helps you track and review; you complete cancellations with the provider or billing platform.
